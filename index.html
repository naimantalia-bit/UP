Campaign for the UP app

Till cash back do us part

A campaign for Upside about the everyday couple who never miss a chance to earn. Six parts, from the big idea to the final video, plus the process behind every choice.

Talia NaimanClient: UpsideOctober 2026
Part 5 · Video production

The finished film

How it was made

Characters and world were designed in Midjourney, prepared for animation in ChatGPT and Photoshop with a character sheet and moodboard, and animated shot by shot, mostly in Seedance through Runway, with some shots in Google's model and one in Kling. Edited by hand in Premiere, upscaled to 4K with Topaz Starlight, music in Suno and final finishing in After Effects.

The UI layer

All on screen UI and copy was built as code and rendered frame by frame over the film: a car tracked with OpenCV, notification, claim card, live map, the $4.50 earn counter, kinetic closer and end card. Gilmer, Inter Tight, the book wordmark and the disclaimer on every offer frame.

Part 1 · Campaign platform

The big idea

Big idea
Till cash back do us part

Every couple has one person who will go to any length so nothing slips by. In our world that person runs across the lawn in a robe and rollers because her husband is about to fill up without claiming the offer. Upside turns that small household drama into a vow: you never let cash back go unclaimed again.

Key message
Claim. Pay like always. Earn.

Real cash back on the gas, groceries and dinners you already buy, with no receipts and no change to how you pay. Up to 25¢/gal on gas, $1B+ already earned by Upside users.

Who it's for

Value minded drivers and households, 25 to 55, who fill up every week and shop and eat out locally. They are not coupon hunters. They want a small win that takes no effort.

Why it fits Upside

The brand voice is neighborly, trustworthy and clever. Humor from real life, real people and a simple three step product story deliver all three, and they stand apart from a category full of fear driven "gas prices" ads.

The thread

A thin Pulse green line runs through every asset and carries you from one moment to the next. It echoes the cyclical arrow in Upside's icon: money that leaves you and comes back.

Part 2 · Static ads · 1080×1080

Three ads, three visual approaches

One campaign world, three treatments: lifestyle photography from the film, typography as the hero, and Upside's own vector illustration language. All three share Gilmer Medium headlines, the book wordmark, the Pulse accent and the disclaimer, so they read as one campaign in the feed.

Why three approaches

Each treatment wins a different kind of attention. Photography carries the film's humor and real people, typography stops the scroll with a joke you read in one second, and illustration explains the product with Upside's own visual language. Testing all three tells us which world to invest in.

Part 3 · Variations

Five per ad, generated from one spec

Each ad was turned into a template that keeps the message and brand fixed, and each variation changes what you see: a different moment from the film, a different composition of the same type idea, or a different scene in the illustration world, plus hook copy and colourway. One renderer draws every file, so grid, type and logo cannot drift, and every variation passes an automatic brand check for banned words, spelling and the disclaimer. Each one changes a variable worth testing, which also gives Meta's delivery system genuinely different creatives.
